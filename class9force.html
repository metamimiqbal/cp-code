01 · Introduction

What is Force?

Definition: Force is an external agent that can change the state of rest or motion of a body, or change its shape and size. It is a vector quantity — it has both magnitude and direction.

বাংলায়: বল হলো এমন একটি বাহ্যিক কারণ যা কোনো বস্তুর বিরামের অবস্থা বা গতির অবস্থার পরিবর্তন করতে পারে বা পরিবর্তনের চেষ্টা করে।
F = m × a Force = Mass × Acceleration  |  SI Unit: Newton (N)  |  1 N = 1 kg·m/s²

⚡ Effects of Force

  • Can start motion in a stationary object
  • Can stop a moving object
  • Can change direction of motion
  • Can change speed of a moving object
  • Can change shape/size of an object

📏 SI Unit

The SI unit of force is Newton (N).

1 Newton is defined as the force that gives a mass of 1 kg an acceleration of 1 m/s².

1 N = 1 kg·m·s⁻²

📐 Vector Nature

Force is a vector quantity. This means it needs both:

  • Magnitude — how much force
  • Direction — which way

It can be represented by an arrow. The length shows magnitude, the arrowhead shows direction.

02 · Classification

Types of Force

🌍

Gravitational Force

The attractive force between any two objects having mass. Earth pulls all objects towards its center.

F = G · m₁m₂ / r²

G = 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²

🔌

Electromagnetic Force

Force between charged particles. Includes both electric and magnetic forces.

  • Like charges repel, unlike charges attract
  • Responsible for chemical bonds
  • Much stronger than gravity
🤸

Applied Force

Force applied directly to an object by a person or another object. Examples: pushing a door, pulling a rope.

🧲

Magnetic Force

Force exerted by magnets on other magnets or magnetic materials. Can attract or repel.

🏊

Normal Force

Perpendicular force exerted by a surface on an object resting on it. Opposes the weight of the object.

🪢

Tension Force

Force transmitted through a string, rope or cable when pulled tight. Always pulls — never pushes.

03 · Fundamental Laws

Newton's Three Laws of Motion

I

Law of Inertia

Statement: An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion with the same speed and direction, unless acted upon by an unbalanced external force.

বাংলায়: কোনো বস্তু বাহ্যিক বল প্রয়োগ না করলে তার বিরামের বা গতির অবস্থা পরিবর্তিত হয় না।

🎬 Animation: Ball in Motion

No friction → ball moves forever (ideally)

Inertia is the tendency of an object to resist changes in its state of motion. The greater the mass, the greater the inertia.

Real-life Examples:
  • Passengers jerk forward when bus brakes
  • Coin on a card falls in glass when card is pulled
  • Tablecloth trick
  • Seat belts save lives
Types of Inertia:
  • Inertia of rest — resistance to starting motion
  • Inertia of motion — resistance to stopping
  • Inertia of direction — resistance to changing direction
II

Law of Acceleration

Statement: The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass. Direction of acceleration is the same as the net force.
F = m × a    ∴    a = F/m    ∴    m = F/a F = Force (N) | m = Mass (kg) | a = Acceleration (m/s²)

🎬 Animation: Same Force, Different Mass

Light mass (m=1kg) → faster

1kg

Heavy mass (m=3kg) → slower

Key relationships:

  • Double the force → double the acceleration (mass constant)
  • Double the mass → half the acceleration (force constant)
  • A 1 Newton force gives 1 kg mass an acceleration of 1 m/s²
III

Law of Action & Reaction

Statement: For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. Forces always come in pairs — they act on different objects.

বাংলায়: প্রতিটি ক্রিয়ার একটি সমান ও বিপরীত প্রতিক্রিয়া আছে।

🎬 Animation: Collision — Action & Reaction

🔵 A →
← B 🔴

Both balls exert forces on each other during collision — equal in magnitude, opposite in direction.

Examples:
  • 🚀 Rocket: gas pushes down → rocket goes up
  • 🏊 Swimming: hands push water back → body moves forward
  • 🔫 Recoil of a gun when fired
  • Walking: foot pushes ground back → ground pushes you forward
Important Note:
  • Action and reaction are always equal
  • They act on different bodies
  • They act simultaneously
  • They do not cancel each other

🌍 Acceleration due to Gravity (g)

Near Earth's surface, the acceleration due to gravity is approximately:

g = 9.8 m/s²

  • Decreases with altitude

05 · Friction

Friction Force

Definition: Friction is the force that opposes the relative motion (or tendency of motion) between two surfaces in contact. It acts parallel to the surface and opposite to the direction of motion.
BLOCK
→ Applied Force
← Friction

Static Friction

Acts when object is at rest. Maximum static friction is greater than kinetic friction.

f_s ≤ μ_s × N

μ_s = coefficient of static friction

Kinetic Friction

Acts when object is in motion. Remains approximately constant during sliding.

f_k = μ_k × N

μ_k = coefficient of kinetic friction

Rolling Friction

Occurs when one surface rolls over another. Much less than sliding friction — reason for wheels!

f_r = μ_r × N

μ_r ≪ μ_k ≪ μ_s

✅ Advantages of Friction

  • We can walk without slipping
  • Braking of vehicles
  • Writing with a pen/pencil
  • Nails hold wood together
  • Matches light due to friction

❌ Disadvantages of Friction

  • Causes wear and tear of machine parts
  • Heat energy loss in engines
  • Reduces efficiency of machines
  • Noise in bearings
Factors affecting friction:
  • Nature of surfaces (roughness)
  • Normal force (weight of object)
  • Does NOT depend on: area of contact, speed of sliding
06 · Pressure

Pressure & Area

Definition: Pressure is the force per unit area acting perpendicular to a surface.
P = F / A Pressure (Pa or N/m²) = Force (N) / Area (m²)  |  SI Unit: Pascal (Pa)  |  1 Pa = 1 N/m²

Large Area → Low Pressure

e.g. Snowshoes spread weight

Small Area → High Pressure

e.g. Needle pierces easily

Fluid Pressure

Acts in all directions

💧 Liquid Pressure

Pressure in a liquid at depth h is:

P = h × ρ × gh = depth (m) | ρ = density of liquid (kg/m³) | g = 9.8 m/s²

Key properties of liquid pressure:

  • Increases with depth
  • Acts equally in all directions
  • Depends on density of liquid
  • Does NOT depend on shape of container

Applications:

  • Dams are thicker at the bottom
  • Hydraulic brakes & jacks
  • Blood pressure measurement
  • Submarines design
Atmospheric Pressure = 101,325 Pa ≈ 1 atm ≈ 760 mmHg The weight of air column above 1 m² of Earth's surface
07 · Momentum

Linear Momentum

Definition: Momentum is the product of the mass and velocity of an object. It is a vector quantity in the direction of velocity.
p = m × v Momentum (kg·m/s) = Mass (kg) × Velocity (m/s)

🎬 Animation: High Mass vs High Velocity

Both can have equal momentum: p = mv. A fast light ball = a slow heavy ball in terms of momentum.

🔒 Law of Conservation of Momentum

Statement: The total momentum of a closed system remains constant if no external forces act on it.

m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂  (before = after collision)

Applications:

  • 🚀 Rocket propulsion
  • 💥 Collisions in sports
  • 🔫 Recoil of gun
  • ⚽ Billiard balls

Impulse:

J = F × t = Δp

Impulse = Change in momentum
Unit: N·s = kg·m/s

🔄 Centripetal Force & Circular Motion

F_c = mv² / r = mω²rF_c = centripetal force | v = speed | r = radius | ω = angular velocity

Centripetal force is always directed towards the center. Examples: Earth orbiting Sun, car turning, satellite in orbit.

08 · Equilibrium

Force Equilibrium

Definition: A body is said to be in equilibrium when the net force acting on it is zero and there is no net torque. The body is either at rest or moving with constant velocity.

🎬 Animation: Balance

Unequal weights → unbalanced

Static Equilibrium

Object is at rest. Net force = 0, Net torque = 0.

  • Book resting on table
  • A balanced seesaw
  • Building structure
ΣF = 0  &  Στ = 0

Dynamic Equilibrium

Object moves at constant velocity. Net force = 0.

  • Car moving at steady speed
  • Parachutist at terminal velocity
  • Airplane in level flight
ΣF = 0  &  a = 0

⚖️ Conditions for Equilibrium

First Condition: The vector sum of all forces must be zero:

ΣF_x = 0    and    ΣF_y = 0

Second Condition (Rotational): The sum of all torques about any point must be zero:

Στ = 0    where    τ = F × d (perpendicular distance)
09 · Vector Analysis

Force as a Vector

Since force is a vector, we need to add forces using vector addition, not simple arithmetic.

📐 Parallelogram Law

If two forces act at a point, the resultant is the diagonal of the parallelogram formed.

R = √(A²+B²+2AB cosθ)

🔺 Triangle Law

If two forces can be represented by two sides of a triangle taken in order, the third side represents the resultant.

📊 Resolution

Any force can be split into perpendicular components:

F_x = F cosθ
F_y = F sinθ
